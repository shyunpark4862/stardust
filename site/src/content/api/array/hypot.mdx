`hypot(x1, x2)` computes $\sqrt{x_1^2 + x_2^2}$ for each pair of elements: the length of the hypotenuse of a right
triangle with legs $x_1$ and $x_2$, or the distance of the point $(x_1, x_2)$ from the origin. Together with
[`atan2`](/api/array/atan2/), which gives the angle, it converts Cartesian coordinates to polar coordinates.

The computation does not form the squares $x_1^2$ and $x_2^2$ directly, so it neither overflows nor underflows where
they would, and `hypot` is preferable to `sqrt(x1**2 + x2**2)`.

The result is always `float64`; `bool` and `int64` operands are converted to `float64` first (see
[functions with floating-point results](/api/array/dtypes/#functions-with-floating-point-results)). Complex operands
raise `TypeError`; [`abs`](/api/array/abs/) gives the magnitude of a complex number.

## Examples

Lengths of the hypotenuse of three right triangles:

```python
>>> import stardust as sd
>>> sd.hypot(sd.asarray([3.0, 5.0, 8.0]), sd.asarray([4.0, 12.0, 15.0]))
array([ 5.0, 13.0, 17.0], dtype=float64)
>>> sd.hypot(3, 4)
5.0
```

Large and small values. The squares overflow to infinity or underflow to 0, but `hypot` does not:

```python
>>> big = sd.asarray([1e200, 3e-200])
>>> sd.hypot(big, big)
array([1.414213562373095e+200, 4.242640687119285e-200], dtype=float64)
>>> sd.sqrt(big**2 + big**2)
array([inf, 0.0], dtype=float64)
```

## Notes

An infinite operand gives `inf`, even when the other operand is NaN; otherwise a NaN operand gives NaN.

```python
>>> sd.hypot(sd.asarray([sd.inf, sd.nan, sd.nan, -sd.inf]), sd.asarray([sd.nan, sd.inf, 1.0, 0.0]))
array([inf, inf, nan, inf], dtype=float64)
```
